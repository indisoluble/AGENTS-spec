# 01 — Behavior

Behavior turns requester intent, constraints, and feedback into product objectives and an accepted requirements baseline for design and evaluation. The requester or an authorized delegate decides what becomes accepted product intent. Discovery keeps proposed changes and examples for review.

- To understand why an outcome matters, start with the [objectives](deliverables/baseline.md#established-objectives) and their [rationale](deliverables/baseline.md#rationale-map). O1 takes priority over O2.
- To understand an accepted requirement, find it under [behavioral requirements (BR1–BR10)](deliverables/baseline.md#accepted-behavioral-requirements) or [self-contained delivery (PR1)](deliverables/baseline.md#established-product-requirement). Check the [established constraints](deliverables/baseline.md#established-constraints) and follow the requirement's design links for its current realization.
- To consider a behavioral change, check the [baseline](deliverables/baseline.md) for related requirements, then review [proposed examples by requirement](deliverables/discovery.md#requirement-to-scenario-coverage) and [open formulation questions](deliverables/discovery.md#formulation-questions-and-evidence-limits). All behavioral examples remain proposed.
- To trace an earlier proposal, see the [disposition of the earlier candidates](docs/candidate-and-example-history.md#disposition-of-the-earlier-candidates) and the [baseline's acceptance provenance](deliverables/baseline.md#status-and-provenance).
