# Open product questions

These questions came from reviewing [proposed examples](example-coverage.md#sources-and-decision-scope). Each describes a choice about what a requester should experience. The requester or an explicitly authorized delegate can settle the outcome and separately decide whether a particular example defines it.

## Which activities count as inquiry?

An agent normally reads relevant project material before choosing a fix. If material uncertainty still blocks that choice, the requester is due prompt notice of the uncertainty and why it matters, unless it was already communicated. The open issue is **when preparation becomes an investigation**. That boundary determines when notice is due and when the agent must bound the investigation and reassess an inconclusive step.

Suppose a question already blocks progress, and an existing architecture record might answer it. Should the agent be able to read relevant records before giving notice, however central the question is? Or should the purpose of the work matter, so that using a record to investigate a known blocker can call for notice even while reading? Specify what separates ordinary preparation from investigation in that case. Either outcome must respect authorization and limits. Notice alone grants no permission.

The proposed examples vary the presence of a blocker, the purpose of a check and the source of the answer.

- [BR4.4](proposed-examples/bounded-inquiry.md#br44--ordinary-checking-does-not-require-an-inquiry-notice) involves ordinary reading or a regression check with no blocking question.
- [BR4.6](proposed-examples/bounded-inquiry.md#br46--a-prescribed-procedure-can-still-be-inquiry) uses the same benchmark to choose an approach or to validate an already settled limit.
- [BR4.7](proposed-examples/bounded-inquiry.md#br47--reading-existing-project-documents-remains-context-gathering) varies the source of the answer. Today's contract treats reading existing project documentation as gathering context even when a decision is blocked. The example records that choice for comparison.

The accepted notice and reassessment duties are recorded in [BR4](../../02-formulation/deliverables/baseline.md#br4--bounded-investigation-and-timely-reassessment).

## When does an expectation about effort call for another decision?

A requester says they expect a small fix. A second local correction now looks necessary and is otherwise authorized. Should the agent continue after reassessing the evidence, or are there circumstances where the requester expects to decide before that extra work? What words or circumstances would distinguish an estimate from an intended limit? The distinction matters when work grows but its objective, direction and risk stay the same.

An explicit cap or checkpoint already requires direction before crossing it. Material uncertainty about the requester's intent or permission must also go back to the requester before dependent work. Compare [BR3.4, an explicit limit](proposed-examples/authorization-and-communication.md#br34--do-not-pass-a-checkpoint-or-enlarge-a-limit-on-your-own) with [BR3.8, an expectation of a small fix](proposed-examples/authorization-and-communication.md#br38--distinguish-an-expectation-from-a-binding-limit). The latter illustrates the current permission to continue within authority when the expectation alone sets no limit. The accepted authorization boundaries are recorded in [BR3](../../02-formulation/deliverables/baseline.md#br3--autonomy-within-authorization-and-prior-direction).

## Recording a decision

Record the outcome, its conditions and any remaining uncertainty using the [Discovery handoff](../docs/maintaining-discovery.md#pass-a-decision-to-formulation). The [acceptance account](../../02-formulation/docs/acceptance-record.md) identifies the scope of earlier decisions. The current contract continues to apply until an authorized change.
