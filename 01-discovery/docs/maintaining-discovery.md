# Maintaining Discovery

## Investigate a behavioral question

1. Describe a situation in which the answer matters. Who is acting, what decision do they face, and what would the requester experience? Identify the [affected objective](../deliverables/objectives-and-rationale.md) and where the question came from. For feedback from later work, record what was tried, under which conditions, and what was observed or found infeasible.
2. Try a concrete example, then vary a condition that could change the answer. Consider the requester’s desired result, what an implementer would have to know, and what an evaluator could observe that distinguishes success from failure. Try a counterexample or exceptional case where two interpretations predict different results. One requester can examine all three perspectives, with an agent helping identify assumptions or alternatives.
3. For an existing decision, check the cases against its agreed scope, permissions and exceptions. The [baseline](../../02-formulation/deliverables/baseline.md) records the accepted wording and constraints. Ask whether a different mechanism could satisfy the same requirement. If an accepted source already decides the outcome, link to it. If plausible answers still differ, explain which condition matters, what each answer would require, and what evidence or requester direction is missing. Keep a material conflict or unanswered choice in [open questions](../deliverables/open-questions.md), with links to the relevant examples. A gap in a fixture or a defect in a mechanism can return to its owner without opening a product decision.

For example, a requester may already know that a regression check is running. Compare that with an agent investigating a blocker the requester has never heard about. Would the requester need to hear about the uncertainty before a benchmark? Before reading an architecture record that might answer it? These [inquiry examples](../deliverables/open-questions.md#which-activities-count-as-inquiry) isolate when notice would make a difference.

When several rules or cases emerge, use Example Mapping to pair each proposed rule with cases that illustrate or challenge it. Keep unanswered questions alongside them. A conversation or short notes can suffice. For an unfamiliar methodological issue, start with Cucumber and follow the project's fallback and departure policy. ## Revise an objective or its rationale

Identify the requester need and the authorized direction for the desired outcome or its priority. Feedback, observed costs, and constraints help explain why an outcome matters and what limits it. An observation about one candidate can motivate a proposal. Establishing a new priority requires a decision about that priority. If the decision is missing, keep the proposed change open.

In [objectives and rationale](../deliverables/objectives-and-rationale.md), state the outcome, its qualifications, and the source and scope of the direction. Check the affected rationale entries against the linked requirements in both directions. Explain how each requirement contributes and keep any uncertainty about benefits visible. For a change confined to the means of achieving an outcome, check the Design reference first. Change objective wording only where the outcome, rationale, or description of that mechanism is actually affected.

## Add or revise a proposed example

- Start with the situation and the outcome to explore. For a case illustrating an existing requirement, find its [subject suite or matrix entry](../deliverables/example-coverage.md). State the starting facts, the action or event, and the observable result in the language of the task. Include only the details needed to distinguish the behavior. For a permission, show a permissible choice. For a recommendation, preserve its exceptions.
- Use the [classification definitions](../deliverables/example-coverage.md#reading-a-proposed-example). Cite the accepted requirement and the applicable design or contract source at the group or case that relies on it. If the outcome depends on a design-only detail, identify the detail beside the case. Link an open question when the case distinguishes unresolved product outcomes. If a mechanism explains how a Direct outcome is achieved, keep that explanation in a separate design note when it matters.
- Preserve an existing ID when revising its case. For a new case under an indexed requirement, use the next unused suffix in that requirement’s suite group. Add its heading link once in the matching Direct or Detail review column. Add a link under “Also exercised by” only where the case actually exercises another requirement. Update suite ranges and counts, classification totals, and affected question or clarification references. Check known summaries that quote the count, such as the [evaluation model](../../05-evaluations/docs/evaluation-model.md#purpose-and-evaluation-model). Check links to a renamed heading even when its ID is stable. Explore a new outcome in open questions with an illustrative case. Assigning a BR number requires an accepted requirement.

## Check the effect of a mechanism change

Compare the changed Design prescription and its contract clause with the accepted requirement. Read the examples that cite the source. If the observable example still follows from the requirement, an obsolete design note, citation, or group qualification may be the only text to update. If the proposed outcome itself assumes the old mechanism, revisit its conditions, classification and any related open question. Preserve its proposal status and identify any successor. A correction to the authoritative source belongs with its owner within the authorized work.

For example, [BR7.1](../deliverables/proposed-examples/authorization-and-communication.md#br71--frame-substantial-work-without-creating-an-approval-gate) illustrates initial framing without an approval checkpoint. Its separate design note identifies the current outline, bounds, and chat channel. If only the channel changed, check the note and its source against the authorized Design change. The Direct outcome could still illustrate the same requirement.

Look up the affected ID, heading, or path in the matrix, question, suite, and known direct dependents. A baseline reference governs what a proposal may treat as accepted. A clause reference traces why a detail appears. A README link helps a reader find it. Compare the meaning for the first two and follow the actual link for the third. Reconcile classifications and totals with the suites, and check that no edit has silently changed a permission, exception, or decision status. ## Pass a decision to Formulation

A question is ready for a decision when the requester can see which outcomes differ, for whom, under which conditions, and what choosing one would leave open. If the evidence is still insufficient, name the missing fact. Once the requester or an authorized delegate decides, link the proposal and examples to the decision, identifying the version, decision maker, scope, conditions, exceptions, and residual uncertainty. Keep provisional IDs traceable. If the decision covers only part of the question, leave the rest open.

[Formulation](../../02-formulation/README.md) needs the agreed rule, its source, and which examples, if any, the decision expressly accepts as defining. Link back from the Discovery question to the resulting baseline entry. Agreement on an outcome alone leaves its illustrative examples proposed. Evaluations can then derive an independent expectation from accepted intent through the [test-first iteration](../../docs/pipeline.md#test-first-iteration).
