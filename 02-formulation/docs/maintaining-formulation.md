# Maintaining Formulation

## Establish what the decision covers

Start with the [Discovery handoff](../../01-discovery/docs/maintaining-discovery.md#pass-a-decision-to-formulation). Read the proposal and its distinguishing cases alongside the relevant [objective and rationale](../../01-discovery/deliverables/objectives-and-rationale.md), the [existing requirement and constraints](../deliverables/baseline.md), and the cited evidence or open question. Agreement with a current contract clause can explain a proposal’s wording. Product acceptance requires a decision about the outcome.

Identify the requester or authorized delegate, the version they reviewed, and the outcome, conditions, exceptions, and examples their decision covers. Make the decision traceable from the entry. Several rules can share one source, as BR1–BR10 share the [owner-reported acceptance account](acceptance-record.md#behavioral-acceptance-and-editorial-provenance). Distinguish the editor’s interpretation from what the source establishes about the decision maker’s reasons.

For a partial decision, formulate the settled outcome and link the remaining choice to its [Discovery question](../../01-discovery/deliverables/open-questions.md). A decision selecting a procedure for the current contract belongs in Design. Design feedback or an unexpected run can reopen a behavioral question. Revising accepted intent takes a decision under the same [product authority](../../docs/authority.md#development-decisions).

## Write or amend the rule

1. Identify the actor or artifact, the circumstances that trigger the rule, and an outcome a reader could observe. Ask what a satisfying response and a violation would look like under the same conditions. Vary a condition to expose a permission or exception. If the decision leaves it unclear whether a response is allowed, take that distinction back to Discovery.
2. State the rule in the vocabulary of the task. The baseline uses EARS (Easy Approach to Requirements Syntax) to connect conditions to required responses. Use a state or event where it matters. Keep exceptions close and use additional sentences when needed. Preserve the difference between a duty, a permissible choice, and a recommendation with justified departures. EARS is the project’s specification technique. BDD uses concrete examples to establish shared understanding. [EARS forms used in the project](../../03-design/deliverables/mechanisms/knowledge-and-documentation.md#ears-making-required-behavior-explicit) · [Cucumber on Formulation](https://cucumber.io/docs/bdd/#formulation-what-it-should-do)
3. Keep the requirement’s ID when amending it. Give a newly accepted outcome its own baseline entry and ID if no existing requirement covers it. Compare what the decision and the proposed wording require, recommend, permit, or prohibit, for whom and when. Check interacting rules, priorities, and applicable constraints, including their measurement and exceptions. An editorial clarification preserves what would satisfy or violate the existing rule. A newly required response, an earlier deadline, or a lost exception needs authority for that change.

For example, read [BR3](../deliverables/baseline.md#br3--autonomy-within-authorization-and-prior-direction) against two actions by an agent considering a new consequential architectural direction. Obtaining direction before adopting it satisfies the consultation duty when the specific choice lacks prior authorization. Disclosing the choice afterwards addresses a separate duty and cannot supply prior authorization. Extending that same mandatory timing to *every* unsettled material choice would also change BR3, which gives other choices a qualified ask-first recommendation. The [R2 rationale](../../01-discovery/deliverables/objectives-and-rationale.md#r2) explains the interest in requester control.

Keep essential qualifications with the rule. Link its rationale and acceptance source from the baseline. Ask whether a different mechanism could yield the same permitted outcomes under the same constraints. For instance, [BR6](../deliverables/baseline.md#br6--learning-from-observed-results) requires learning from results. A proposed replacement for PDSA belongs in Design if it preserves that outcome, other affected rules, and the [method-selection constraints](../deliverables/baseline.md#method-selection-qualifications). Compare the [selection reasons and alternatives](../../03-design/deliverables/methods-and-references.md#choosing-or-replacing-methods). The current prescription binds until an authorized change. Selection and implementation each need the corresponding authority.

## Determine whether an example defines the rule

A useful example gives the relevant starting facts, an event or action, and a visible result. Include the fact that distinguishes two plausible responses. For a permission, allow the choices the rule permits. Given/When/Then can make that distinction readable. The wording alone establishes neither agreement, executability, nor coverage. [Cucumber on concrete examples](https://cucumber.io/docs/bdd/examples/)

| What you have | How to use it |
| --- | --- |
| An exploratory Discovery example | Follow its ID to the proposed facts and outcome. Both Direct and Detail review are proposal classifications. |
| An expressly accepted defining example | Record the agreed version and its conditions beside the requirement in Formulation. Cite the decision that accepted that example as defining. Link the Discovery ID to the resulting entry so its origin and any outstanding parts remain traceable. Acceptance of the rule alone leaves the example proposed. |
| A Design illustration | Use it to understand or review the selected mechanism. Its steps can be specific to that design. |
| An executable Evaluations case | Inspect its fixture, assertions, and rubric to see what it can distinguish. What actually happened comes from the identified run and its reviewed evidence. |

Consider [BR4.7, reading an architecture record to answer a blocking question](../../01-discovery/deliverables/open-questions.md#which-activities-count-as-inquiry). The current design classifies that reading as context gathering. If a requester decision settles when that blocking question triggers notice and bounded investigation, record the decided scope under BR4. Identify separately whether the decision accepts BR4.7 itself as defining. Leave other unsettled exclusions open. Check BR3’s consultation and BR7’s reporting duties, then trace the effect to the [inquiry mechanism and §2.4](../../03-design/deliverables/mechanisms/bounded-inquiry.md). Report any mismatch for authorized work. The product decision by itself grants no implementation permission.

## Hand accepted intent to Evaluations

For a [requirements-led iteration](../../docs/pipeline.md#test-first-iteration), hand off the accepted requirement and version, applicable conditions, and any expressly accepted defining example. Evaluations uses the accepted rule to [establish the expectation](../../05-evaluations/docs/maintaining-evaluations.md#establish-the-expectation) and define a criterion that distinguishes satisfaction from violation before implementation, independently of Candidate wording. That check guides authorized Design and Candidate work. Evaluations then assesses the identified candidate. Formulation owns the intent and Evaluations owns the operational criterion.

Before claiming that an agent met a BR in a case, trace the criterion to that accepted intent. Identify the case and evaluator versions, candidate digest, conditions actually exercised, observations, assertion results, and any necessary human judgment. In the [requester-guidance case](../../05-evaluations/deliverables/cases/requester-guidance.md#human-review-and-interpretation), a final response can be present even if the agent guessed the missing timezone. Read the response and workspace evidence to judge whether the agent sought direction and deferred the dependent change. PR1 instead permits [standalone contract inspection](../deliverables/baseline.md#r5).

Today all Discovery examples remain proposed and requirement-to-evaluation traceability is incomplete. Existing behavioral cases exercise contract clauses. E1–E40 govern the evaluator, and passing its tests establishes only the machinery behavior checked. A claim about agent behavior is limited to the assessed conditions. Attributing the result to the contract requires comparative evidence. ## Verify the edit and report gaps

Compare the edited baseline with the decision and the previous version, including adjacent qualifications and the rules it references. Follow the BR or PR identifier in the [Design clause index](../../03-design/deliverables/contract-design.md#contract-clause-index) to the affected mechanism and Candidate clauses. Separately inspect any evaluation expectation that depends on the changed condition. A decision governs the rule, a clause link traces its realization, and a README link helps a reader find it. Each needs a different check. [Reading a reference](../../docs/traceability.md#reading-a-reference)

Check affected example references, incoming paths, fragments, and labels from the stage README. Use text comparison for an editorial edit, standalone inspection for delivery content, and case evidence for a behavioral claim. Report any missing decision, unexercised condition, or out-of-scope implementation or coverage gap with its owner and the evidence still needed.
