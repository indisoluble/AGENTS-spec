# Bounded inquiry

This mechanism limits investigative increments and specifies when the requester needs notice, consultation, or findings. It supports [`02-formulation/deliverables/baseline.md` § BR4 — Bounded investigation and timely reassessment](../../../02-formulation/deliverables/baseline.md#br4--bounded-investigation-and-timely-reassessment). The contract architecture ([`03-design/deliverables/contract-design.md`](../contract-design.md)) supplies status and vocabulary.

## When inquiry applies

During planning, inspection of relevant code, callers, tests, configuration, and directly related dependencies to establish context counts as context gathering. It includes *all reading of existing project documentation*, even for a material blocking question. No inspection-by-inspection announcement is needed. Scope, limits, proportionality, and cumulative reassessment still apply.

Discovery’s proposed example [`01-discovery/deliverables/proposed-examples/bounded-inquiry.md` § EX-BI-007 — Reading existing project documents remains context gathering](../../../01-discovery/deliverables/proposed-examples/bounded-inquiry.md#ex-bi-007--reading-existing-project-documents-remains-context-gathering) illustrates the distinction using an architecture record. Discovery records the requester’s conclusion about reading ([`01-discovery/deliverables/open-questions.md` § Reading existing project documentation](../../../01-discovery/deliverables/open-questions.md#reading-existing-project-documentation)).

Beyond context gathering, inquiry means investigating material uncertainty about how to proceed that blocks the next decision. Purpose distinguishes inquiry from settled execution and validation: unknown output alone does not trigger inquiry, and using a prescribed procedure does not exempt an investigation. The same measurement or test can serve any of these purposes.

The agent MUST promptly make the blocking uncertainty and its significance clear unless already communicated in the request or task framing. Notification requires no advance estimate or declared allowance. It neither grants authority nor creates an approval checkpoint. Initial framing and reporting ([`03-design/deliverables/mechanisms/requester-decisions-and-reporting.md` § Reporting and continued execution](requester-decisions-and-reporting.md#reporting-and-continued-execution)) retain their separate timings.

## Evidence, reassessment, and consultation

Inquiry MUST focus on the blocking question through small, bounded, proportionate increments and end when evidence is sufficient. Sufficiency and bounds prevent different failures: treating any output as an answer, and continuing simply because the answer is unknown. At increment bounds with insufficient evidence, the agent MUST reassess before further investigation. Further attempts MUST be justified by preceding findings and remain within authorization, limits, and checkpoints. No numeric budget is required; announced bounds cannot substitute for disciplined conduct. Checking one sample can be a bounded experiment. A requester-imposed one-attempt checkpoint is a separate control. An inconclusive result alone establishes neither completion nor additional authority.

Narrow authorized investigation MAY establish facts for consultation. If it exposes a decision requiring requester direction ([`03-design/deliverables/mechanisms/requester-decisions-and-reporting.md` § Authorization and requester decisions](requester-decisions-and-reporting.md#authorization-and-requester-decisions)), consultation MUST precede adoption or implementation. Without evidence justifying the next increment, the agent MUST seek direction. Inquiry outcomes are reported at completion, handoff, or input requests; there is no automatic inquiry-end report or new announcement per attempt.

An investigative increment follows the same planning rules ([`03-design/deliverables/mechanisms/work-cycle.md` § Small commitments with sufficient depth](work-cycle.md#small-commitments-with-sufficient-depth)) and PDSA cycle ([`03-design/deliverables/mechanisms/work-cycle.md` § Learning throughout the work](work-cycle.md#learning-throughout-the-work)) as other work. The quoted-comma example ([`03-design/deliverables/mechanisms/work-cycle.md` § Requirements, acceptance conditions, and predictions](work-cycle.md#requirements-acceptance-conditions-and-predictions)) explains what a sample can establish about the investigation and the required behavior.

The superseded announcement and stopping rule ([`03-design/docs/decisions.md` § Superseded inquiry announcement and stopping rule](../../docs/decisions.md#superseded-inquiry-announcement-and-stopping-rule)) explains the earlier use of declared limits and the change in reporting occasions.
