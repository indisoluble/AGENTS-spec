# Contract design review

Use these editorial checks and [28 non-normative illustrations](#assessing-design-changes) to review how the current design is explained. They add no runtime duties, accepted requirements, evaluator criteria, or approval step. [Proposed BDD examples](../../01-discovery/deliverables/example-coverage.md#proposed-example-suites) remain separate. [Shared authority](../../docs/authority.md#status-of-engineering-records) governs those distinctions.

## Reviewing contract explanations

Use the [cross-stage change guide](../../docs/changing-the-contract.md#change-the-owner-and-verify-its-dependents) for maintenance boundaries and [contract design](../deliverables/contract-design.md) for mechanisms and rationale. The checks below concern explanation and comprehension, not behavioral evidence.

### Reader paths

The reference reader knows Git and coding agents. A junior developer should be able to adopt the contract without studying its methods first; “power users” seek practical depth rather than possessing method expertise.

| Document | Reader task |
| --- | --- |
| [README.md](../../04-candidate/pre_README.md) | Understand the offering, ordered objectives (token control, then requester awareness/control), benefits and limits; install with replacement precautions, check adoption, and try a first task. It should enable adoption alone, concisely and accessibly, without exaggerated claims. |
| [README-powerusers.md](../../04-candidate/pre_README-powerusers.md) | Evaluate/use the contract through formal, detailed practical guidance. Start with an ordinary outcome-oriented request and show agent handling, then situations, conditions, limitations, exceptions, and examples. Reference detail must be findable without preceding the first example; this remains user documentation. |
| [Contract architecture](../deliverables/contract-design.md) | Understand selected mechanisms, interactions, reasons, trade-offs, and decision status. [Formulation](../../02-formulation/README.md) locates accepted intent and [Discovery](../../01-discovery/README.md) locates proposals; [shared architecture](../../docs/architecture.md) explains engineering relationships. |

Use plain Markdown, descriptive headings, direct links, and comparison tables. Introduce terms before use and methods where they explain action or choice. Present default behavior before optional requester controls, and the workflow before its activities. Connect request, decision trigger, context/evidence, action, result, and next step; phase names and definition links alone do not explain that path. Read examples without relying on the editing conversation.

Neither guide nor Design documentation has a numeric length target or limit. Detail and useful repetition serve understanding; assess navigation and the combined impression of headings/examples, keeping consequential qualifications near claims. Avoid empty categories, obligatory templates, and formalism without explanatory value. The contract's [standing-context constraint](../../02-formulation/deliverables/baseline.md#portability-and-standing-context-qualifications) applies separately to `AGENTS.md`.

### Explanatory ownership and evidence

Guides and design can explain the same subject at different depths for distinct reader needs under the [summary boundary](../../docs/documentation.md#choosing-the-owner). Link to canonical detail rather than copying complete procedures, reference-assignment tables, or lifecycles; necessary copies retain owners/update relationships. The practical guide owns requester-facing explanations; mechanism documents own design rationale.

The [practical CSV walkthrough](../../04-candidate/pre_README-powerusers.md#from-a-request-to-a-result) owns shared scenario facts; the [technical walkthrough](../deliverables/mechanisms/work-cycle.md#how-the-design-works-in-a-task) uses them to explain design choices and stays aligned. Alternative paths state their assumptions. Illustrative choices and editorial checks cannot become policy.

Match claims to their sources:

- Intended outcomes and accepted behavior are recorded in the [established objectives](../../01-discovery/deliverables/objectives-and-rationale.md#established-objectives) and the [requirements baseline](../../02-formulation/deliverables/baseline.md). Keep candidates and proposals distinct.
- Current operating rules: [delivered contract](../../04-candidate/pre_AGENTS.md), with the [clause index](../deliverables/contract-design.md#contract-clause-index) for detail/rationale. Current design remains binding without detailed product acceptance, and authorized redesign may replace it.
- Methods: operational contract meaning and [reference assignments/consultation boundaries](../deliverables/methods-and-references.md#principles-and-reference-boundaries), without importing whole frameworks or stronger source rhetoric. Clear rules need no routine method research.
- Evaluator claims: [E1–E40](../../05-evaluations/README.md#evaluator-requirements), applicable case interpretation, and captured contract/rubric.
- When a change depends on how a provider [discovers instruction files](../deliverables/delivery-and-portability.md#discovery-of-staged-instruction-files) or loads them, assess those claims against the applicable official documentation and the behavior observed in the target environment.

Do not imply that contract text overrides higher-priority instructions, grants permissions, bypasses approvals, or technically guarantees compliance.

### Meaning and evidence checks

Review the complete reader task, including headings and walkthroughs:

1. **Purpose:** fulfill the audience's task, explain ordered objectives and knowledge continuity, and preserve unique explanations at useful depth without promoting selected methods into product requirements.
2. **Semantics:** preserve actor, modality, trigger, timing, scope, authorization, conditions, exceptions, permissions versus obligations, and cross-clause interactions. Check limits versus expectations, autonomy versus consequential direction, notification versus consultation, and context/execution/validation versus inquiry. Preserve bounded conduct, evidence sufficiency, reassessment, and the already-communicated notification exception without inventing allowances or approval gates.
3. **Examples:** identify assumed facts, illustrative choices, expected outcomes, and observations. Explain why the evidence supports a choice, what completes the step, and what could change the next decision. Additional work alone does not trigger approval; absent architecture grants no unrestricted design authority. Organizing an explanation creates no additional process.
4. **Continuity:** retain distinct framing, notification, advance-disclosure, and consolidation timings. Routine-report recommendations are neither a ban nor a per-increment duty. Handoffs transfer results, authorization/limits including consumed allowances, direction, next-step bounds/conditions, evidence, and uncertainty rather than activity logs.
5. **Evidence/delivery:** distinguish intended effects from demonstrated results; text consistency proves neither comprehension, behavioral equivalence, nor savings. Check Markdown, paths, anchors, incoming references, and [release-relative meanings](../../docs/architecture.md#staged-sources-and-release-relative-references). Consumer artifacts retain no engineering dependencies, including navigation. Apply artifact-specific criteria and keep transient measurements/validation records in working or review records.

| After reading | The reader should explain |
| --- | --- |

| [From a request to a result](../../04-candidate/pre_README-powerusers.md#from-a-request-to-a-result) | Initial framing, why that increment, completion evidence, and findings informing the next authorized step. |
| [Pausing or continuing](../../04-candidate/pre_README-powerusers.md#when-the-agent-pauses-or-continues) | Informing/consulting/reporting, prior-direction triggers, binding limits, useful updates without per-increment reports, and handoff state. |
| [Investigation](../../04-candidate/pre_README-powerusers.md#when-an-investigation-is-needed) | Inquiry versus context/validation, already-communicated uncertainty, bounds without announced allowances, evidence sufficiency/reassessment, and notification versus consultation. |

For reader testing, ask members of the reference audience to perform/explain these tasks in their own words. Distinguish inability to find an answer from misunderstanding it, and observed comprehension from editorial prediction. Keep observations in working/review records; editorial review alone does not establish usability.

## Assessing design changes

**Non-normative illustrations of the current implementation, pending requirements-led review.** All 28 distinctions below are retained separately from accepted requirements, proposed BDD defining examples, executable checks, and behavioral results. They establish neither comprehensive coverage nor an evaluation procedure. Future review must decide each illustration's role in requirement clarification, evaluation expectations, design explanation, or history; no promotion is performed here. The [baseline acceptance account](../../02-formulation/deliverables/baseline.md#behavioral-acceptance-and-editorial-provenance) identifies the accepted outcomes. Discovery tracks the [unsettled details](../../01-discovery/deliverables/open-questions.md), and the [evaluation model](../../05-evaluations/docs/evaluation-model.md#purpose-and-evaluation-model) owns behavioral evidence limits.

Assess self-containment by standalone reading, and preservation or deliberate change of clause meaning by comparing strength, trigger, scope, conditions, and exceptions. The [clause index](../deliverables/contract-design.md#contract-clause-index) locates detailed mechanisms; the delivered contract remains runtime authority.

| Scenario | Design distinction to assess |
| --- | --- |
| Broad objective, including a new project | The agent limits its next commitment and keeps later work at outline level until detail is needed for the next decision. Necessary depth, affected dependencies, and validation remain within scope; the breadth of the goal does not justify an unconstrained first increment. |
| Starting a task | After proportionate preliminary context gathering, assess whether the agent briefly communicates the task outline and first increment's objective and bounds before substantial work. The statement creates no approval checkpoint. It can also communicate material inquiry uncertainty and its significance, satisfying that notification without a second message. |
| Requester expectation or binding limit | “I expect a small fix” is not automatically a budget; “consult before a second increment” is a checkpoint. Material ambiguity about the intended force needs clarification. Only requester direction can revise requester-imposed limits or checkpoints; subdivision and retries do not reset them. |
| Ordinary authorized change | The agent selects a small increment and completes its checks before completing the increment, even without a chat report. Completion grants no additional permission or automatic documentation artifact; another increment can proceed under existing authorization unless a checkpoint requires a pause. |
| Completed exploration without a record trigger | Without durable material findings or significant lasting choices, no record is required merely to log the discussion. Transient task facts, such as a temporary CI outage or a failed experiment relevant only to the current attempt, follow the reporting rules only. |
| Material findings or significant proposals during review | A [finding](../../04-candidate/pre_AGENTS.md#32-durable-findings-and-permanent-records) needs a permanent record only when it is both material and durable, even if it comes from a discarded approach; a missing owner leads to the minimum record rather than backfill, and a prohibited write is reported as unfinished work. A concrete significant proposal ready for decision or review requires an [ADR](../../04-candidate/pre_AGENTS.md#33-significant-decisions-adrs) retaining the evidence that materially shaped it rather than routine PDSA history; brainstorming or a finding alone does not. Status reflects existing decision authority. Recording supplies no implementation authority or new approval checkpoint. |
| Verified factual documentation error during assessment | The agent may make an existing description of current project facts within the assessed scope match the canonical owner of that kind of knowledge, subject to scope, requester restrictions, §3.8, and tool permissions. Requirements, decision records, and protected instruction files are excluded. A verified mismatch with authoritative package configuration can justify correcting a README command; it does not authorize changing implementation, intended requirements, accepted decisions, or protected instruction files. |
| Task-relevant debt found during assessment | Evidenced debt may be recorded after considering accepted requirements and trade-offs. Any entry triggers classification-uncertainty and chat-disclosure duties. The general material-findings requirement still applies; neither classification nor recording authorizes a fix. |
| Requester or tool prohibits writes | Assessment permissions remain subject to requester restrictions (§2.1) and §3.8. An explicit ban on creating, modifying, or deleting files covers records and factual documentation corrections; tool restrictions also remain binding. Findings and proposed text can be supplied in chat within those limits, but chat does not satisfy a permanent-record obligation; the unmade record is reported as unfinished work. |
| Continued execution and reporting | Several increments can complete without a report for each one. At task completion, handoff, or when seeking input, the agent consolidates unreported work. Routine progress reports should be minimized and intermediate communication should be concise and useful. Assess a routine update against those recommendations and any requester or tool instructions; its presence alone is not an automatic violation. Required findings and records are maintained throughout. |
| Requested or required disclosures | Disclosures affecting authorization or a decision requiring requester input must precede affected work. Other information, including inquiry outcomes, should normally join the next consolidated report. Initial task framing precedes substantial work, and material inquiry uncertainty and its significance must be made clear promptly unless already communicated in the request or task framing. Reporting or notification alone creates no approval checkpoint and cannot replace required consultation. |
| Selecting and revisiting work | Plan connects the requested outcome, permission, relevant context, and evidence to a useful next increment from the first request. Assess whether that connection is understandable, including when missing evidence or consequential constraints change the next step. Study and Act inform later planning; later work stays at outline level until detail is needed and revisions stay within authorization. |
| Investigation contradicts its prediction | An evidenced answer can complete the investigation while disproving the prediction; unmet implementation behavior remains incomplete. |
| Reading existing project documentation | Reading remains context gathering without inspection-by-inspection announcements, even for a material blocking question. Inspecting relevant code, callers, tests, configuration, and directly related dependencies to establish context is also context gathering. Scope, requester limits, proportionate work, and cumulative reassessment apply to all of it. |
| Prescribed empirical operation or acceptance check | Unknown output alone does not establish inquiry. Contrast executing a settled measurement/calculation or checking acceptance with using the same procedure to investigate an unsettled approach. Classify purpose before assessing inquiry duties; a procedure neither automatically triggers nor exempts inquiry. |
| Material uncertainty about how to proceed blocks the next decision after context gathering | Promptly make the uncertainty and its significance clear unless already communicated in the request or task framing. Private planning or a retrospective report cannot replace a required timely notification. Assess whether the investigation itself is bounded and proportionate. The notice requires no prescribed format or advance effort allowance. |
| The request already communicates inquiry uncertainty and its significance | No duplicate inquiry notification is required. Initial task framing, bounded conduct, sufficient evidence, cumulative reassessment, and any required consultation still apply. Coverage by the request does not authorize every consequential direction that the inquiry might suggest. |
| Inquiry exposes an unsettled consequential direction | Narrow investigation may establish facts for consultation within existing authorization. Obtain requester direction before adopting or implementing a choice requiring consultation under §2.2; merely notifying the requester or presenting evidence does not satisfy that requirement. |
| Investigation obtains sufficient evidence or reaches an increment's bounds | End inquiry when evidence is sufficient. At an increment's bounds with insufficient evidence, reassess before further investigation without claiming completion. Further attempts need justification from preceding findings and must respect authorization, limits, and checkpoints; a new advance announcement is not automatic. Report outcomes at §3.7's occasions and seek required direction before affected work. Neither an exhausted attempt nor a new increment supplies authority or resets a requester limit. |
| Acceptance-critical verification is unavailable | Sufficient alternative evidence can establish the same condition unless a specific check is required. Otherwise the condition remains unverified and validation incomplete unless validly revised. Disclosure alone does not satisfy it. |
| Equivalent evidence exists for an available acceptance check | The relevant available check must run. Equivalence alone does not permit substitution; the unavailable-check exception does not apply. |
| Known risk without an internal-quality compromise | Preserve the known risk under topic 11 without inventing a technical-debt classification. |
| Documentation-only work | Applicable methods and validation apply without requiring code or unit tests for their own sake. |
| Additional work | Reassess cumulative progress, remaining work, direction, and limits before expanding planned work or starting another increment/attempt. Each next increment needs evidence supporting its contribution and must remain within existing authorization, direction, and limits. |
| New consequential design direction or material departure | Obtain requester direction before adoption or implementation unless the specific choice is already authorized, including in a new project without established architecture. A Proposed ADR can prepare the decision; recording it or reporting later cannot supply prior authorization. |
| Handoff and resumption | Transfer meaningful results and continuation state: task objective and established direction; applicable limits/checkpoints and consumed allowances; next or unfinished increment objective/bounds/acceptance conditions; and relevant inquiry conclusions, supporting evidence, and unresolved uncertainty. A chronological activity log is insufficient. Use that state on resumption; recover missing authorization or limit state or obtain direction before dependent work. |
| Any tool fails to load the contract | The package defines no separate bootstrap stop/report/resume policy. Neither a bridge nor a README supplies one, and an unloaded contract cannot govern the failure. Use actual tool and requester instructions and distinguish loading diagnostics from demonstrated application. |
| Root and directory instruction files | Only the three root-relative contract/bridge paths receive special edit protection. Subtree rules may be added only when compatible with the root contract; the tool decides discovery, scope, and precedence, which never makes conflicting policy acceptable; encountered conflicts are reported. |
