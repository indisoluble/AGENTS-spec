## Purpose and evaluation model

The suite examines coding-agent behavior in small, controlled situations drawn from [AGENTS.md](../../03-contract/prerelease_AGENTS.md). The contract prioritizes token control through bounded, proportionate work, then informed requester control of consequential direction, supported by durable project knowledge. Every case receives a fresh temporary workspace with its fixture and an unchanged copy of `03-contract/prerelease_AGENTS.md` as workspace-root `AGENTS.md`. Ten cases are registered: the smoke case checks the infrastructure; the nine behavioral cases exercise specific contract decisions rather than general compliance.

A run connects four responsibilities:

1. **Execution:** Codex receives the case's task and works in the disposable fixture.
2. **Observation:** the launcher retains execution evidence and, for behavioral cases, initial/final workspace state. Cases requiring human review also capture the final response.
3. **Automated assessment:** Pydantic Evals applies the case's assertions to the observed result. Independent-execution and contract-change-followthrough first run their evaluator-owned functional checks after agent cleanup and before workspace removal.
4. **Human assessment:** a reviewer uses the retained rubric, messages, commands, and workspace evidence to assess criteria automation cannot establish.

An agent's completion claim is not proof of success. Automated assertions, infrastructure health, task completion, and human judgments answer different questions; [results](running-and-reviewing.md#results-evidence-and-failure-investigation) explains how they are reported. A successful observed behavior does not establish that AGENTS.md caused it. Causal attribution requires comparative evidence.

The [catalogue](../cases-and-requirements.md#cases-and-interpretation) describes designed coverage and its limits. A case deliberately presents a condition and defines its assessment; this does not mean an agent has passed. Executed evidence requires a run, reviewed results require assessment, and broader confidence requires repeated, varied evidence. There are no dedicated scenarios for initial planning communication, cumulative reassessment, requester limits across increments, consequential architectural consultation, or handoff/resumption continuity. Conditional rubric criteria do not ensure those situations occur; the [bounded-inquiry interpretation](../cases-and-requirements.md#bounded-inquiry) also identifies notification and retry coverage limits. Full PDSA assessment, containers, and CI remain deferred. The CLI does not collect human verdicts or resume an agent with an answer.

The accepted product baseline remains in [01-behavior/baseline.md](../../01-behavior/baseline.md); the [60 behavioral examples](../../01-behavior/discovery.md#proposed-example-suites) remain proposed in `01-behavior/proposed-examples/`. [E1–E40](../cases-and-requirements.md#verifiable-requirements) specify evaluator requirements, not product requirements. The current cases retain their historical contract-clause-based interpretation; moving their documentation does not make them requirements-derived acceptance tests or complete BR1–BR10 acceptance coverage. Evaluation results provide evidence for human review, not permission to change product intent, contract design, or evaluator acceptance criteria. [Cross-domain traceability and feedback](../../docs/authority-and-change.md#cross-domain-traceability-and-feedback) owns these authority relationships and the remaining traceability work.
