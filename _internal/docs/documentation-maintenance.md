# Documentation maintenance for AGENTS-spec

Use this file when editing the repository's documentation. It defines ownership, a short editing workflow, and the checks needed to preserve meaning. It sits outside the ordinary path for learning about or using [AGENTS.md](../../contract/prerelease_AGENTS.md) and adds no operating obligations to that contract.

### Check the authority or evidence for each kind of statement

Engineering changes follow the established objectives, PR1, and product constraints in [product-requirements.md](../../requirements/docs/product-requirements.md), the transferred design in the [contract architecture](../../requirements/docs/architecture.md) and remaining design prescriptions and retained material in [contract-design.md](../../requirements/docs/contract-design.md), applicable accepted decisions, and requester authority under the [engineering authority model](../../docs/architecture.md#development-time-and-runtime-authority). Runtime obligations remain those actually delivered through [AGENTS.md](../../contract/prerelease_AGENTS.md); internal documentation cannot silently change a consuming agent's obligations.

For descriptions of current behavior, use the [current-semantic specification](../../requirements/docs/architecture.md#current-semantics-vocabulary-and-coverage) as the engineering reference and check runtime claims against the delivered `AGENTS.md`. Preserve actor and modality; trigger and timing; scope and authorization; conditions and exceptions; permissions versus obligations; and relevant cross-clause interactions. Missing separately accepted requirements do not prevent documenting current semantics, make existing contract obligations optional, or reduce a prescribed mechanism's current normative force.

When editing a guide, check each statement against the authority, reference, or evidence that governs that kind of statement:

Let each answer its reader's question, and link to the fuller treatment where useful. Avoid copying complete procedures, reference-assignment tables, or lifecycle descriptions between guides; necessary copies need a clear owner and update relationship. ### Public guides and internal engineering documents

The two consumer guides are user-facing and serve different reading needs; the [reader paths](#reader-paths) describe how to write each.

- **[README.md](../../contract/prerelease_README.md)** introduces the specification to potential adopters. It should capture their interest, convey what the contract offers and why someone might adopt it, state its main benefits and expectations accurately, give an accessible way to get started, and invite further reading. Keep it concise, accessible, and engaging; it is neither an internal engineering document nor an exhaustive reference. Engaging presentation never justifies exaggerated, unsupported, or misleading claims.
- **[README-powerusers.md](../../contract/prerelease_README-powerusers.md)** is the more formal, detailed guide for people evaluating or already using the specification: its practical behavior and important distinctions, how to work with agents under it, and the relevant conditions, limitations, exceptions, and examples. It is more precise and extensive than the introduction but remains user documentation, not an internal engineering reference.

Because their readers differ, the two guides may intentionally discuss the same concepts, expectations, examples, or benefits. The introduction should be useful without following a link for every important concept, and the practical guide may revisit those concepts with greater precision. Such overlap is a reader-oriented summary of the kind the contract's [canonical-knowledge rule](../../contract/prerelease_AGENTS.md#35-canonical-knowledge-and-affected-documentation) permits, not by itself a defect to remove or extract. It is not an exemption from that rule: `AGENTS.md` remains the authority for operating rules, both guides must preserve its rule strengths, conditions, and exceptions, and neither may define requirements independently, maintain a competing authoritative definition, or contradict another owner. The shared-subject table above still assigns which section owns each detailed explanation.

The five staged files at the `contract/` root belong to the user-facing product or its adoption and usage material. They contain no references or dependencies into engineering documentation, including `_internal/` and `requirements/`, even for optional further reading. Engineering documents may reference those staged files. Engineering navigation belongs in [_internal/README.md](../README.md). The [accepted boundary decision](../../docs/architecture.md#requirements-led-engineering-and-productinternal-separation) records the scoped application of §3.6 using the historical root layout: the consumer README identifies user-facing locations, and the internal README serves the engineering project.

The consumer guides remain independently useful for adoption and practical use. They explain technical aspects of the contract that help their readers, such as engineering techniques, architecture documentation, requirements and verification, decision records and technical debt, and instruction-file discovery and compatibility limits. Keep explanations self-contained at each reader's depth, or link to the relevant `AGENTS.md` clause, other product documentation, or an external reference. Removing an internal link must not remove essential practical information.

Documents under `docs/`, `_internal/`, `requirements/`, and `evaluations/` serve people who engineer, evaluate, evolve, operate, and maintain the specification and its evaluation framework. Their readers must know where each fact is changed, so apply ownership strictly: each detailed technical fact, requirement, interface, procedure, architectural explanation, or significant decision has one identifiable owner, and other documents link to it or give a concise summary for their own readers. [_internal/README.md](../README.md), for example, summarizes the subsystem and links to its owners without owning their contents.

To tell a permissible summary from a problematic copy, ask whether it answers its own reader's question, agrees with its owner while keeping qualifications whose omission would mislead, links to the owner where readers need more, and could be corrected from that owner without separate maintenance. A copy that readers could treat as authoritative, or that must be updated independently to stay correct, creates a second owner. For example, the consumer README's “Bounded investigations” expectation and the practical guide's [investigation section](../../contract/prerelease_README-powerusers.md#when-an-investigation-is-needed) both explain inquiry at different depths, which is intentional while each preserves the contract's conditions. Restating requirements E1–E40 or module responsibilities in `_internal/README.md` would instead duplicate [evaluations.md](../../evaluations/docs/evaluations.md), so that README links to it.

## Editing workflow

1. **Identify the change and its owner.** Separate editorial clarification from changed operating behavior. Authorization for repository development changes, including edits to the staged contract and bridges, derives from developer direction and accepted engineering decisions under the [development authority model](../../docs/architecture.md#development-time-and-runtime-authority). The candidate contract's [protected-file rule](../../contract/prerelease_AGENTS.md#38-instructions-protected-files-and-external-actions) protects instruction files in consuming repositories; it does not govern maintenance of AGENTS-spec. Identify behavior changes explicitly; a README cannot enact them.
2. **Update the authoritative owner first.** For a clarification of current operation, identify the relevant contract clauses and rationale, then edit the explanation in its owner. For a future authorized behavior change, follow `contract-design.md`, established product requirements and constraints, applicable accepted engineering decisions, and requester authority; reflect the change consistently in the runtime contract and affected canonical engineering documentation, with evaluation changes where affected. An implementation edit alone does not establish intent or authorize revised expectations. Preserve each rule's meaning and qualifications when behavior is unchanged. Acceptance of RC1–RC10 and traceability to the contract and evaluations remain later work.
3. **Follow its dependencies.** Within authorized scope, update affected summaries, examples, references, and bridge explanations in the canonical owners and known direct dependents. Links warrant checks, not necessarily edits; check known direct incoming links when headings or paths move. This does not authorize searches for unknown dependents, repository-wide audits, or backfills. If a known external link would break, consider retaining its Markdown heading with a brief link to the new location.
4. **Walk through the reader's task.** Use the paths below to check progression and discoverability. For a substantial rewrite, maintain a temporary map from old content to its new location, recording why any material is removed, so unique explanations do not disappear.
5. **Review the complete result.** Apply the checklist below. Use relevant [design scenarios](../../requirements/docs/contract-design.md#assessing-design-changes) to check explanations against the contract. If the contract changes, also apply the technical guide's [portability criteria](../../requirements/docs/contract-design.md#keeping-the-contract-portable).

Keep current descriptions and useful lasting rationale in their owners. Significant historical reasoning, accepted decisions, and influential rejected or superseded alternatives remain in their canonical design or decision records, with status and replacement links. Transient revision narratives, draft measurements, package fingerprints, and temporary content maps belong in working notes or delivery records. Historical PDSA material may explain earlier intent but does not override current decisions or implementation. Retain only enduring ownership guidance here. A shorter entry point must not be achieved by quietly dropping necessary explanations from the repository.

## Reader paths

The reference reader is a developer already familiar with Git and coding agents. A junior developer should be able to follow basic adoption without first studying the contract's engineering methods. Build on that existing knowledge to explain the contract's specific definitions, decisions, and trade-offs.

- **[README.md](../../contract/prerelease_README.md) — basic guide:** explain the ordered objectives—bounded token use, then informed requester control—give actionable installation steps and a useful first task, and show essential expectations and further reading. A reader new to the contract should be able to adopt it using this guide alone.
- **[README-powerusers.md](../../contract/prerelease_README-powerusers.md) — practical guide:** begin with an ordinary outcome-oriented request and show how the agent handles it. Then explain situations the user may encounter. Put detailed reference material where readers can find it without making it a prerequisite for the first example.
- **[requirements/README.md](../../requirements/README.md) — requirements entry point:** explain desired-outcome responsibility independently of contract mechanisms and evaluator requirements, distinguish established and provisional status, and link to current authoritative definitions. Describe current ownership and remaining migration work without copying definitions.
- **[product-requirements.md](../../requirements/docs/product-requirements.md) — established product requirements:** define the established objectives, PR1, and product constraints with their original qualifications, status, and provenance. Link to the retained design explanations without turning selected mechanisms or provisional candidates into established requirements.
- **[requirement-candidates.md](../../requirements/docs/requirement-candidates.md) — provisional requirement candidates:** retain RC1–RC10, their evidence references, qualifications, provenance, and unresolved acceptance questions. Link to the shared classification explanation and detailed design evidence without accepting current mechanisms as enduring requirements.
- **[requirements/docs/architecture.md](../../requirements/docs/architecture.md) — operating-contract architecture:** explain the transferred shared vocabulary and contract-clause coverage index as glossary and crosscutting concepts, and design goals and boundaries, layer interaction, and continuation as solution strategy and runtime view, preserving their current design status, rationale, authorization qualifications, and evidence limits. Engineering-system architecture and development authority stay in `docs/architecture.md`.
- **[contract-design.md](../../requirements/docs/contract-design.md) — remaining contract design:** link to the established requirements, provisional candidate, and contract-architecture owners and distinguish current binding design prescriptions, rationale, trade-offs, and classification and evidence. Preserve remaining design knowledge and sufficient method and reference detail for assessing the contract. Instructions for maintaining the guides stay here.

Use plain Markdown only, descriptive headings, direct links, and tables for comparisons. Introduce terms before depending on them; explain methods where they clarify an action or design choice. Present default behavior before optional requester controls.

When explaining a workflow, give the overall path before developing individual activities. Show what prompts a decision, what context or evidence informs it, and how the result affects what follows. Naming phases or linking to their definitions does not by itself explain these connections. Read examples from the reader's starting point, without relying on knowledge from the editing conversation.

There is no numeric length target or limit for the three guides. Reducing their byte count is not a goal; detail, examples, and useful repetition should serve understanding. Assess necessary detail, repetition, navigation, and the combined impression of headings and examples. Accurate sentences still need a reading path that teaches the intended responsibilities. Keep consequential qualifications close to their claims; avoid empty categories, obligatory templates, and formalism that adds no understanding. The contract's separate [portability constraint](../../requirements/docs/product-requirements.md#portability-and-standing-context-qualifications) applies to `AGENTS.md`; its [design explanation](../../requirements/docs/contract-design.md#keeping-the-contract-portable) remains in the technical guide.

## Meaning and evidence checklist

Use these editorial checks for the complete change. The technical guide's [design scenarios](../../requirements/docs/contract-design.md#assessing-design-changes) own the detailed review cases; the contract owns the behavior being explained.

1. **Purpose, coverage, and ownership.** Check that each guide fulfills its reader's purpose and accurately explains the current contract's ordered objectives and selected mechanisms: control token consumption through small, meaningful, bounded PDSA increments and proportionate work, then keep the requester informed and in control of consequential direction. Explain documentation's role in preserving project knowledge for maintainers, users, and future agents. Confirm that necessary explanations remain available at the appropriate depth and that consolidation has not deleted unique knowledge. Keep editorial procedures here and design rationale in the technical guide.
2. **Fidelity when explaining the current contract.** Compare affected claims with the authoritative clauses. Preserve the actor, rule strength, trigger, timing, scope, conditions, and exceptions. Check informal expectations against binding limits, routine autonomy against required consequential consultation, and informational statements against approval checkpoints. Distinguish context gathering, execution, validation, and material inquiry. Inquiry explanations should retain prompt notification of uncertainty and its significance, including the exception when the request or task framing already communicates them, without inventing mandatory five-element announcements, advance effort estimates, or numeric budgets. They should also retain actual bounded conduct, sufficient evidence, reassessment, and requester limits across attempts. Neither notification, an ADR, a report, nor incremental execution creates authorization or replaces required prior direction. A paraphrase, heading, or example must not introduce operating policy.
3. **Connected explanations.** Follow each walkthrough from its starting request through decisions, actions, evidence, and what follows. Can the reader understand how a choice was reached and what could change it? Use the [reader comprehension questions](#reader-comprehension-questions) to review progression and, when testing substantial rewrites with readers, identify what they can explain without coaching. Keep distinctions made by the contract visible; do not imply an extra process merely to organize the explanation.
4. **Examples and qualifications.** Identify assumed project facts, illustrative choices, expected outcomes, and observed results clearly. Keep consequential qualifications close to their claims. Check complete walkthroughs, not only terminology: additional work alone must not be illustrated as an approval trigger, and missing architectural direction must not imply unrestricted design authority. Distinguish a bounded experiment from an optional requester-imposed attempt limit, and justified continuation from open-ended inquiry. Reporting examples should preserve mandatory consolidation at completion, handoff, or input requests, advance disclosures affecting authorization or requester decisions, and the separate timing of initial framing and inquiry notification. Minimizing routine reports and keeping updates concise and useful are recommendations: neither a categorical ban nor a required report for every increment. Other information should normally join the next consolidated report. Handoffs should convey meaningful results and the objective, direction, applicable limits, next-step bounds and acceptance conditions, relevant inquiry conclusions, evidence, and uncertainty needed to continue, not an activity log.
5. **References, attribution, and provider evidence.** Preserve links to primary references and exact attribution using the technical guide's [reference assignments](../../requirements/docs/contract-design.md#principles-and-reference-boundaries). Do not import a whole framework or a reference's stronger rhetoric as policy. Retain original provider-check dates when moving guidance; record a new date only for a newly verified or changed claim. Editorial work does not refresh external evidence.
6. **Evidence for claims.** Match claims about benefits, limits, and compatibility to the available evidence. Distinguish intended effects from demonstrated outcomes. Textual consistency does not establish measured comprehension, equivalent agent behavior, or token savings.
7. **Links and delivery.** Check Markdown, relative paths, heading anchors, incoming links, and delivered file versions against the intended repository layout. Confirm that summaries agree with their owners and staged consumer files contain no references or dependencies into engineering documentation, including `_internal/` and `requirements/`. Apply artifact-specific criteria from their authoritative location when those artifacts change, and keep transient measurements and validation records out of the guides.
