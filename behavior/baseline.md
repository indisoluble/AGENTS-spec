## Status and provenance

Established objectives and product requirements are supported by explicit requester direction or accepted project records beyond the implemented clause alone. Objectives state desired outcomes. **BR1–BR10 and PR1 are all accepted product requirements with equal authority in the same baseline.** BR1–BR10 are behavioral product requirements specifying the behavior expected of coding agents. PR1 specifies a property of the delivered `AGENTS.md` artifact: self-contained operating instructions. Their different subjects and verification methods do not imply different authority or acceptance status. Established constraints retain their original strength, exceptions, measurement, and revision conditions; a preferred target is not a hard ceiling. The source [classification and evidence](../docs/architecture.md#status-and-evidence-boundaries) distinguishes these from selected design mechanisms, current binding prescriptions, provisional candidates, and historical reasoning.

PR1 is verified primarily through inspection of the delivered contract. Verifying BR1–BR10 requires behavioral evidence from agents using that contract; inspecting its text alone does not establish that agents exhibit the required behavior. The separate sections below organize requirements by subject without changing any requirement's scope or strength.

The ordered objectives were already recorded in the design guide and independently in the [historical documentation reader guidance](https://github.com/indisoluble/AGENTS-spec/blob/09fc0a68d0122f4c970932e14760f83cb0b4e46a/_internal/docs/documentation-maintenance.md#reader-paths) and [adoption guide](../contract/prerelease_README.md). The historical link preserves the original provenance; current reader guidance belongs to [contract explanation review](../design/design-review.md#reader-paths). The accepted [requirements-led engineering decision](../docs/architecture.md#requirements-led-engineering-and-productinternal-separation) and the request for the restructuring recorded there establish the self-contained product boundary and distinguish intended behavior from implementation. The existing method-selection and portability constraints were retained under the request to preserve those constraints; that does not accept additional agent behavior inferred from them. Detailed behavioral acceptance cannot be reconstructed merely from an “accepted contract” commit title or normative wording, so it remains provisional where no separate evidence is available.

Acceptance of product intent does not accept every currently implemented contract clause or make a selected mechanism an enduring product requirement. Currently prescribed methods retain their present contractual force; future requirements acceptance and authorized redesign are separate work. The earlier ownership transfers did not accept, reject, or revise RC1–RC10 or the RCx-Py proposals. The source [decision-status index](../design/contract-design.md#selected-design-and-retained-decisions) and [historical source and interpretation](../design/contract-design.md#historical-source-and-interpretation) retain the relevant selected decisions, limited acceptance of wording compromises, superseded alternatives, and original repository provenance. ## Established product requirement

**PR1 — Self-contained operating contract.** The delivered `AGENTS.md` contract shall contain its complete operative instructions without depending on guides, bridge-specific policy, or internal engineering documents for additional operating duties.

This restates the accepted product boundary in verifiable EARS form, rather than accepting every current clause as a requirement. Bridges may still be needed for a provider to load the contract. Guides may explain it, and adopting-project artifacts supply that project's facts, requirements, build commands, and architecture. None supplies hidden generic operating policy. A standalone reading can check textual completeness; loading and actual agent behavior require their own evidence.

## Accepted behavioral requirements

These requirements concern the agent receiving the delivered contract, within the actual instruction hierarchy, requester authorization, tool permissions, and approvals. A duty to investigate, validate, report, or record does not enlarge that authority. Mandatory responses use “shall”; permissions and qualified recommendations retain their distinct strength. The linked design accounts retain the full current mechanisms, not additional hidden runtime instructions. The [coverage matrix](discovery.md#requirement-to-scenario-coverage) relates proposed examples to these requirements; it claims neither exhaustive coverage nor observed compliance.

### BR1 — Focused, proportionate, authorized work

When planning or performing authorized work, the agent shall keep the next commitment focused and proportionate, include necessary inseparable dependencies and validation, and reason sufficiently for the next decision. Saving effort, lines, files, or tokens shall not excuse incorrect, inconsistent, or incomplete work. Before expanding work or starting another increment or attempt, the agent shall reassess cumulative progress, remaining work, established direction, and applicable limits, and justify the next step's contribution from evidence; absent that justification, it shall seek direction.

At review, handoff, or a deliberate pause after a meaningful step, the agent shall leave coherent work. Before an increment that cannot stand alone, it shall explain why, what remains usable or testable, and how to undo it. It shall disclose material limits on reversing proposed or completed changes. A required stop may leave work incomplete; coherence does not permit continuing past that stop or reporting incomplete work as complete. Easy reversal remains a recommendation, not a guarantee that every authorized change is reversible.

Current realization: [small commitments and sufficient depth](../design/contract-design.md#small-commitments-with-sufficient-depth), contract §§1.1, 2.3, and 2.5. Exact decomposition and planning elements remain selected design; no numerical effort quota or uniquely correct sequence is established.

### BR2 — Evidence-based planning and knowledge ownership

When preparing to choose an approach or derive or refine acceptance conditions, the agent shall first identify the authorized objectives and constraints and inspect relevant project evidence. It shall distinguish required behavior and completion conditions from observed behavior, assumptions, inferences, and predictions about an approach. It shall use the owner of each kind of knowledge, report contradictions, and take consequential conflicts that ownership cannot settle to the requester before the dependent choice. Passing code or tests shall not silently redefine intended behavior.

Current realization: [knowledge roles](../design/contract-design.md#documentation-choices) and [requirements, acceptance conditions, and predictions](../design/contract-design.md#completion-predictions-and-bounded-investigation), contract §1.1. The current artifact-role assignments and explicit increment prediction remain binding design; the summary does not establish every category or planning element as a permanent mechanism. No global precedence order among project artifacts is introduced.

### BR3 — Autonomy within authorization and prior direction

Within agreed limits, the agent may choose easily reversible routine local details. It shall stay within requester authorization and actual tool permissions and approvals, disclose material assumptions, and take material unresolved intent or permission questions to the requester. Such assumptions may support only progress with limited, easily reversed effects; they do not supply missing authority. If no safe step is possible, the agent shall present alternatives and seek direction.

Before adopting or implementing a new consequential architectural or lasting design direction, or materially departing from established direction, the agent shall obtain requester direction unless the specific choice is already authorized. Other unsettled material choices retain the current recommendation to ask first, with weighed and justified departures; that recommendation is not a universal mandatory approval gate and its exceptions waive no mandatory duty. Before exceeding requester-imposed limits or passing checkpoints, the agent shall obtain requester direction; increments, retries, and handoffs shall not reset them. Missing answers shall not count as broader permission.

If a consequential choice has been made without required prior direction, the agent shall prominently disclose the choice, reasons, effects, uncertainty, and revision or reversal options. Retrospective disclosure shall not count as authorization.

Current realization: [authorization and requester decisions](../design/contract-design.md#authorization-and-requester-decisions), contract §§2.1–2.3, 2.5, and the [instruction/external-action boundaries](../design/contract-design.md#why-the-bridges-differ) in §3.8. Informal expectations are not automatically binding limits. No agent-created effort-baseline checkpoint is reinstated.

### BR4 — Bounded investigation and timely reassessment

When material uncertainty about how to proceed remains after relevant context gathering and blocks the next decision, the agent shall promptly communicate that uncertainty and its significance unless already communicated in the request or task framing. Investigation shall focus on that question through bounded, proportionate steps within existing authority and limits, and shall end when the evidence is sufficient.

When an investigation step finishes inconclusively, the agent shall reassess what was learned before deciding whether another step is justified by preceding findings. Any further step shall remain bounded and within authorization, limits, and checkpoints. An inconclusive result shall not itself justify continued investigation, expand authority, or establish completion. Decisions requiring requester direction remain subject to BR3; narrow authorized investigation may establish facts for that consultation. Relevant conclusions, evidence, unresolved uncertainty, and missing work shall be reported at BR7's reporting occasions.

Current realization: [context and bounded inquiry](../design/contract-design.md#completion-predictions-and-bounded-investigation), contract §2.4. Its context-gathering exclusions remain in force. Unknown results alone do not trigger inquiry; communication neither authorizes work nor creates an approval checkpoint. No default numerical budget, mandatory advance allowance, automatic stop-and-ask after one attempt, or automatic inquiry-end report is introduced.

### BR5 — Completion supported by validation

When assessing completion at increment or task level, the agent shall require correctness, consistency, and validation across affected artifacts against the applicable requirements and acceptance conditions. The agent shall run relevant available acceptance checks before completing an increment, even when no report is due at that point. Checks shall cover affected behavior, risk, and hard performance or operational limits. The agent shall disclose failed, unavailable, or omitted checks and report conditions without sufficient evidence as unverified and validation as incomplete, unless validly revised under the relevant authority. Disclosure alone shall not satisfy a condition.

A relevant check runnable with current authority, access, resources, and ordinary expected setup remains available even if an easier alternative exists. Sufficient alternative evidence may replace only an unavailable check that was not specifically required. Missing authority, access, resources, or materially greater work shall be disclosed rather than silently obtained or absorbed. Validation failure or implementation difficulty shall not justify weakening tests, requirements, or acceptance conditions to claim success. Revision authority follows the source of the requirement or condition. Completion of an investigation does not establish completion of an unfixed implementation task.

Current realization: [validation and test evidence](../design/contract-design.md#validation-and-test-evidence), contract §1.3. Appropriate tests for behavior changes, repairs, and clarified edge cases remain a SHOULD; Given/When/Then is the current qualified test-writing technique, not a newly permanent framework requirement. Required stops may leave work incomplete.

### BR6 — Learning from observed results

When results provide evidence about an approach, the agent shall use that evidence to retain, revise, or discard the approach and inform subsequent work within authorization. Revising a prediction or abandoning an approach shall not change requirements or acceptance conditions, replace required behavior with learning alone, or authorize another attempt. Material durable findings remain subject to BR8.

Current realization: [learning throughout the work](../design/contract-design.md#learning-throughout-the-work), contract §§1, 1.3, and 1.4. PDSA remains the mandatory current mechanism applied proportionately, without a worksheet, formal experiment, special mode, or separate learning deliverable merely because a method was used. Acceptance of this outcome does not freeze that mechanism against authorized redesign.

### BR7 — Proportionate communication and actionable handoffs

When preparing to begin substantial work, the agent shall, after gathering sufficient initial context, briefly explain its intended approach and first step before starting. This communication shall not create an approval checkpoint unless another rule requires one.

At task completion, handoff, or when seeking requester input, the agent shall consolidate unreported work into an actionable account of changes and reasons, relevant paths, decisions, and canonical links, evidence and checks including failures or omissions, material assumptions, conflicts, risks and choices, and unfinished work. It shall distinguish evidence from inference and step completion from task completion, and explain material departures from recommendations. Disclosures affecting authorization or decisions requiring requester input shall precede the affected work. Communication should fit the task; minimizing routine reports is a recommendation, not a prohibition on useful updates or a report requirement for every step.

When handing off unfinished work, the agent shall transfer the task objective and established direction; applicable limits and checkpoints, including consumed allowances; the next or unfinished step's objective, bounds, and acceptance conditions; and relevant findings, evidence, and unresolved uncertainty. On resumption, it shall use that state and recover missing authorization or limit information, or obtain requester direction, before dependent work. A handoff shall not create a fresh allowance.

Current realization: [reporting and continued execution](../design/contract-design.md#reporting-and-continued-execution), contract §§1.1, 2.3, 2.4, and 3.7. The contract's more detailed planning and framing elements, including the task outline and first increment's objective and bounds, remain selected design at their current strength. Distinct inquiry notification, exact delivery conventions, and tool-limit disclosures retain their current force. No mandatory report template, routine approval interval, or ban on intermediate communication is introduced.

### BR8 — Durable knowledge without expanded editing authority

When a finding is both material and durable project knowledge, the agent shall preserve it with substantiating evidence in its appropriate canonical record, including when the approach was discarded. If no suitable record exists, it shall create only the minimum record needed within its authority. Transient attempt facts belong in reporting; a finding alone shall not trigger a decision record. The agent shall preserve significant in-scope decision history with the decision-shaping evidence, constraints, reasons, consequences, and useful alternatives needed to understand lasting choices, distinguishing proposed, accepted, rejected, and superseded status. Recording shall neither accept a proposal nor authorize implementation.

During analysis or review alone, writes shall stay within the existing contract's narrow permission and actual requester restrictions, tool permissions, and approvals: material durable findings, decision/debt maintenance, and verified corrections to existing descriptions of current facts in the assessed scope. That factual-correction permission excludes requirements, decision records, and protected instruction files. Assessment alone shall not authorize implementation, new or changed requirements, accepting or changing decisions beyond existing authority, unrelated repairs, backfills, or protected instruction-file edits. If writing a required record is prohibited, the agent shall report the unmet duty as unfinished work.

Current realization: [assessment writes and durable records](../design/contract-design.md#authorization-and-requester-decisions), [ADRs](../design/contract-design.md#adrs-preserving-the-reasons-for-lasting-choices), and [technical debt](../design/contract-design.md#recording-technical-debt), contract §§2.1, 3.2–3.4, and 3.8. Debt recording remains optional, with its own evidence, classification, reporting, and maintenance conditions; a separate material-and-durable finding can still require a record. The current ADR duty is triggered when a concrete significant in-scope proposal is ready for decision or review; routine choices and exploratory ideas do not acquire new record duties. ADR form, fields, timing, and arc42 placement remain selected design. No general documentation-write permission is created.

### BR9 — Engineering quality and preservation

When making authorized changes, the agent shall preserve unaffected public contracts, compatibility, error handling, required logging, resource lifecycles, concurrency guarantees, security, and documented invariants, unless their change is intended and authorized. Refactoring shall preserve observable behavior. Intended behavior changes shall consistently update affected requirements, tests, and documentation within the authorized work. Changes shall remain focused; unrelated cleanup or speculative restructuring shall not be added.

Current requirements may justify complexity; speculation alone shall not justify abstractions, parallel implementations, configuration, or optimization. Optimization should have requirements or evidence and explain material complexity and trade-offs. Clarity, maintainability, suitable reuse, canonical ownership, and reversibility remain qualified quality recommendations rather than minimizing line or file counts. Applicable recommendations retain their conditions and justified exceptions.

Current realization: [engineering scope boundaries](../design/contract-design.md#engineering-scope-boundaries), [Simple Design](../design/contract-design.md#simple-design-choosing-what-the-current-solution-needs), [preservation](../design/contract-design.md#component-boundaries-refactoring-and-validation), and [focused techniques](../design/contract-design.md#focused-techniques-and-how-they-work-together), contract §§1.2–1.3. Acceptance does not make every named technique, design pattern, or recommendation a mandatory enduring mechanism.

### BR10 — Affected requirements and documentation

When authorized work establishes or changes behavior required beyond the current task's execution, the agent shall maintain that requirement in verifiable terms at its canonical owner, without duplicating an existing requirement. Task objectives and sample checks shall not become product requirements merely because they were stated.

When knowledge changes, the agent shall maintain its canonical owner and known direct documentation dependents, checking links and changing them only when affected. At review boundaries, it shall resolve stale, conflicting, duplicated, misplaced content and links within authorized editing scope; current-system descriptions shall distinguish existing, proposed, and unfinished work. Affected-document duties shall not authorize searches for unknown dependents, unrelated audits, backfill, or reorganization. Documentation gaps outside authorized editing scope shall be reported or proposed for separate work, not silently repaired. Analysis/review writes remain subject to BR8.

Current realization: [documentation choices](../design/contract-design.md#documentation-choices), [EARS](../design/contract-design.md#ears-making-required-behavior-explicit), [arc42](../design/contract-design.md#arc42-shared-architectural-context-as-the-project-grows), and [Diátaxis](../design/contract-design.md#diátaxis-choosing-content-for-the-readers-need), contract §§3.1, 3.5–3.6. Known-gap proposals, qualified in-scope improvements, requester deferral, and the agreed-need condition for new guides retain their current strength. These methods are selected design, not newly permanent product requirements.

### Method-selection qualifications

These selection criteria remain constraints on future additions or replacements unless explicitly revised. A proposed method change should explain the need it addresses, the accessibility of its required interpretive material, and any overlap or expansion of obligations. ### Portability and standing-context qualifications

The provider evidence supporting the choice retains its recorded **2026-10-04** check date in the [evidence-date owner](../contract/prerelease_README-powerusers.md#evidence-dates). It has not been newly verified by this restructuring; applicable limits still depend on the actual provider and environment. The [design explanation](../design/contract-design.md#keeping-the-contract-portable) retains the provider-source links and rationale for the approximate project target; those sources do not establish a universal provider limit or automatically revise project policy.

The entire `AGENTS.md` has a **strongly preferred target of about 24,000 UTF-8 bytes after normalizing line endings to LF**. Replace each CRLF pair and any remaining CR with LF before counting the whole file's UTF-8 bytes; do not otherwise trim or reformat its contents. LF and CRLF copies of the same content therefore have the same count. Normalized UTF-8 bytes remain the project's canonical measurement.

Exceeding the target is exceptional but acceptable when further reduction would materially compromise normative fidelity or human comprehensibility. Before such excess is accepted, the contract receives a deliberate compression review. That review removes genuine redundancy and low-value explanatory detail before it compresses core control semantics, such as authorization, requester limits, cumulative reassessment, consultation, inquiry, and continuation. Even over target, the contract keeps meaningful headroom below applicable provider and project combined instruction-loading limits, such as the Codex default documented in the [design explanation](../design/contract-design.md#keeping-the-contract-portable), so directory instructions and other context can coexist. The project defines no separate provider-independent ceiling; those applicable combined limits bound the acceptable excess.

Check the normalized byte count after edits. Automated counts can help; automated enforcement is not required. Normalization defines this project's measurement; provider compatibility still depends on the actual delivered files, other loaded instructions, and tool configuration. Preserve useful spacing and headings. Meeting the target does not establish clarity, compatibility, equivalent behavior, agent adherence, or token savings. Vendor changes can prompt a review but do not automatically change the project policy.

The size target concerns the standing contract itself, not the reasoning or work allowed for a task. Operational meaning remains self-contained in `AGENTS.md`; necessary operating semantics must not move into a README to satisfy the target, because that would undermine portability even if the resulting file were smaller. Compactness is therefore subordinate to normative fidelity and usable comprehension, while standing instruction context remains a constrained resource.

The design document retains the [current wording trade-offs](../design/contract-design.md#current-wording-trade-offs) and their limited acceptance: the identified compromises authorize no further loss of precision. New trade-offs, including reduced explicitness without an intended rule change, remain design decisions to identify for requester review, with the rationale specified there. Previous acceptance and matching counts establish neither losslessness nor behavioral equivalence for a further reduction.
