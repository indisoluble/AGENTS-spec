# Design Reference

This companion to [contract design](contract-design.md) preserves the editorial guidance and all 28 non-normative illustrations extracted from the [baseline Design Reference](https://github.com/indisoluble/AGENTS-spec/blob/4e44bd2c03d2e708654f5060705398e630d0dace/requirements/docs/architecture.md#design-reference). Their status and pending review are unchanged; they are not accepted BDD defining examples. Shared authority classification belongs to the [global architecture](../docs/architecture.md#status-and-evidence-boundaries).

### Reviewing contract explanations

These are editorial checks for explanations of the contract and its design, retained from the existing documentation guidance. They add no operating duties, independently accepted requirements, evaluation criteria, or development approval step. The [behavior entry point](../behavior/README.md#working-with-requirements-and-design-documentation) owns the domain editing path; [engineering architecture](../docs/architecture.md#documentation-ownership-boundaries) owns cross-domain and consumer/engineering boundaries. The runtime contract owns the behavior being explained, and the unchanged [design scenarios](#assessing-design-changes) remain non-normative pending later requirements-led review.

#### Reader paths

The reference reader is a developer familiar with Git and coding agents. A junior developer should be able to adopt the contract without first studying its engineering methods. “Power users” means readers seeking practical depth, not presumed expertise in those methods. Build on that starting knowledge to explain the contract's particular definitions, decisions, and trade-offs.

- The staged [README.md](../contract/prerelease_README.md) introduces the offering to potential adopters: what it offers and why someone might adopt it, its ordered objectives of bounded token use then informed requester control, accurate benefits and expectations, actionable installation and replacement precautions, adoption checks, a useful first task, and further reading. Keep it accessible, concise, and engaging without exaggeration or unsupported claims. A new reader should be able to adopt it from this guide alone.
- The staged [README-powerusers.md](../contract/prerelease_README-powerusers.md) provides more formal, detailed practical guidance for people evaluating or using the contract. Begin with an ordinary outcome-oriented request and show how the agent handles it, then explain situations readers may encounter with relevant conditions, limitations, exceptions, and examples. Detailed reference should be findable without becoming a prerequisite for the first example. It remains user documentation rather than an engineering reference.
- The [contract architecture](contract-design.md) explains current semantics, mechanisms, rationale, interactions, trade-offs, and retained decisions with their status and evidence limits. Its opening navigation locates those design views; [behavior-domain navigation](../behavior/README.md) locates established intent and provisional candidates independently of those mechanisms. Engineering-system relationships and development authority remain in root `docs/architecture.md`.

Use plain Markdown, descriptive headings, direct links, and tables for comparisons. Introduce terms before using them, explain methods where they clarify actions or choices, and present default behavior before optional requester controls. Give the overall workflow before its activities, then connect the request, decision trigger, context or evidence, action, result, and next step. Naming phases or linking definitions alone does not explain those relationships. Read examples from the reader's starting point without relying on the editing conversation.

The two consumer guides and the contract architecture (including this Design Reference) have no numeric length target or limit; reducing bytes is not a goal. Detail, examples, and useful repetition serve understanding. Assess necessary detail, navigation, and the combined impression of headings and examples, keeping consequential qualifications close to claims. Avoid empty categories, obligatory templates, and formalism that adds no understanding. The contract's separate [portability constraint](../behavior/baseline.md#portability-and-standing-context-qualifications) applies to `AGENTS.md`; its [rationale](contract-design.md#keeping-the-contract-portable) remains distinct from guide length.

#### Explanatory ownership and evidence

The introduction, practical guide, and design rationale may cover the same subject at different depths under the [permitted-summary boundary](../docs/architecture.md#documentation-ownership-boundaries). Each answers its own reader's question and links to fuller treatment without copying complete procedures, reference-assignment tables, or lifecycles. Necessary copies need an identifiable owner and update relationship. The practical guide owns requester-facing explanations of [limits](../contract/prerelease_README-powerusers.md#when-the-work-grows-unexpectedly), [consultation](../contract/prerelease_README-powerusers.md#when-the-agent-asks-you), [inquiry](../contract/prerelease_README-powerusers.md#when-an-investigation-is-needed), and [communication and handoffs](../contract/prerelease_README-powerusers.md#when-the-agent-pauses-or-continues); their design rationale stays in the corresponding [authorization](contract-design.md#authorization-and-requester-decisions), [inquiry](contract-design.md#completion-predictions-and-bounded-investigation), and [reporting](contract-design.md#reporting-and-continued-execution) sections in the contract architecture. The [decision-record explanation](../contract/prerelease_README-powerusers.md#preserve-significant-choices-and-identify-debt) similarly serves practical use, while [ADRs](contract-design.md#adrs-preserving-the-reasons-for-lasting-choices) explains the design rationale.

The [practical CSV walkthrough](../contract/prerelease_README-powerusers.md#from-a-request-to-a-result) owns the shared scenario facts; the [technical walkthrough](contract-design.md#how-the-design-works-in-a-task) explains design choices using them and stays aligned when those facts change. Alternative paths and additional examples state their own assumptions. An illustrative choice is not a general requirement, and an example or editorial checklist cannot become a second source of policy.

Check each claim against its kind of authority or evidence: intended outcomes against [established requirements](../behavior/baseline.md) while keeping [candidates](../behavior/discovery.md) provisional; current operating rules against the delivered [AGENTS.md](../contract/prerelease_AGENTS.md), using the [semantic index](contract-design.md#current-semantics-vocabulary-and-coverage) for detail and rationale; adopted methods against their operational contract meaning and [reference boundaries](contract-design.md#principles-and-reference-boundaries); and evaluator claims against [E1–E40](../evaluations/docs/evaluations.md#verifiable-requirements), case interpretation, and the run's captured contract and rubric. Missing independently accepted requirements neither prevents documenting current semantics nor makes current prescriptions optional. Fidelity in an explanation does not require a separately authorized redesign to retain a mechanism.

Tool discovery and application claims use current official provider documentation, rather than an interpretation of the contract. Preserve the recorded [provider evidence dates](../contract/prerelease_README-powerusers.md#evidence-dates) and the separate [staging check date](../docs/architecture.md#staged-sources-and-release-relative-references) when moving guidance; record a new date only for a newly verified or changed claim. Editorial work does not refresh evidence. Do not imply that the contract overrides higher-priority instructions, grants permissions, bypasses approvals, or technically guarantees compliance. Primary method links and exact attribution stay with the [reference assignments](contract-design.md#principles-and-reference-boundaries); neither a whole framework nor a reference's stronger rhetoric becomes policy. Clear contract meaning needs no routine external method research; material ambiguities retain the narrow consultation and reporting boundaries defined there.

#### Meaning and evidence checks

Review the complete change, including headings and walkthroughs, against the authoritative clauses and indexed explanations:

1. **Purpose and preservation.** Each guide should fulfill its reader's purpose and accurately explain the ordered objectives and current selected mechanisms: small, meaningful, bounded PDSA increments and proportionate work, then requester awareness and consequential control. Explain how canonical documentation preserves facts and reasons for maintainers, users, and future agents. Keep unique explanations available at the appropriate depth without treating selected mechanisms as independently accepted requirements.
2. **Semantic fidelity.** Preserve actor, rule strength, trigger, timing, scope, authorization, conditions, exceptions, permissions versus obligations, and cross-clause interactions. Keep informal expectations distinct from binding limits, routine autonomy from consequential consultation, and informational statements from approval checkpoints. Context gathering, execution, validation, and material inquiry remain distinct. Inquiry explanations retain prompt uncertainty/significance notification and its already-communicated exception, actual bounded conduct, sufficient evidence, reassessment, and requester limits across attempts; they introduce no mandatory five-element announcement, advance effort estimate, or numeric budget. Neither notification, an ADR, a report, nor incremental execution supplies authorization or replaces required prior direction.
3. **Connected examples.** Make assumed project facts, illustrative choices, expected outcomes, and observed results explicit. Follow the full path from request through decisions, actions, evidence, and what follows: can the reader explain why a choice was reached and what could change it? Additional work alone is not an approval trigger; missing architectural direction is not unrestricted design authority. Distinguish bounded experiments from optional requester-imposed attempt limits, and justified continuation from open-ended inquiry. Do not imply an extra process solely to organize the explanation.
4. **Communication and continuity.** Preserve mandatory consolidation at completion, handoff, or input requests; advance disclosures affecting authorization or requester decisions; and the distinct timing of initial framing and inquiry notification. Minimizing routine reports and keeping useful updates concise are recommendations, neither a ban nor a required report per increment. Other information should normally join the next consolidated report. Handoffs convey meaningful results, objective and direction, applicable limits, next-step bounds and acceptance conditions, relevant inquiry conclusions, evidence, and uncertainty needed to continue, rather than an activity log.
5. **Claims and delivery.** Match benefit, limitation, and compatibility claims to evidence, distinguishing intended effects from demonstrated outcomes. Textual consistency establishes neither measured comprehension, equivalent agent behavior, nor token savings. Check Markdown, relative paths, heading fragments, known incoming links, and delivered versions against the intended layout, including [release-relative exceptions](../docs/architecture.md#staged-sources-and-release-relative-references). Summaries agree with their owners; consumer artifacts contain no engineering dependencies, including optional navigation. Apply changed artifacts' criteria from their owners and keep transient measurements and validation records in working or review records.

#### Reader comprehension questions

These editorial questions assess whether guides teach their intended responsibilities. They complement the unchanged non-normative [design scenarios](#assessing-design-changes) and supply neither extra agent duties nor behavioral evaluation evidence.

| After reading | The reader should be able to explain |
| --- | --- |
| [README.md](../contract/prerelease_README.md) | What the two objectives are, which files to install, how to check adoption, and what token control can and cannot promise. |
| [From a request to a result](../contract/prerelease_README-powerusers.md#from-a-request-to-a-result) | What the initial plan communicates, why the agent selected that increment, what completes it, and how findings inform the next authorized step. |
| [When the agent pauses or continues](../contract/prerelease_README-powerusers.md#when-the-agent-pauses-or-continues) | How informing, consulting, and reporting differ; which choices require prior direction; how binding limits differ from informal expectations; why useful intermediate updates are allowed without a report per increment; and what results and state a handoff preserves. |
| [When an investigation is needed](../contract/prerelease_README-powerusers.md#when-an-investigation-is-needed) | How inquiry differs from context gathering and validation; when the request or framing already supplies notification; why no announced allowance is needed for effort discipline; what ends inquiry or requires reassessment; and why notification cannot replace consultation. |
| [Use requirements and documentation](../contract/prerelease_README-powerusers.md#use-requirements-and-documentation) | How canonical records preserve shared knowledge, which record triggers apply, and why recording a decision neither authorizes it nor replaces required consultation. |

For reader testing, give readers from the [reference audience](#reader-paths) the relevant guide and ask them to carry out or explain these tasks in their own words. Record inability to find an answer separately from misunderstanding an explanation. Keep observations in working or review records and distinguish observed comprehension from an editor's prediction; editorial review alone does not establish usability.

### Assessing design changes

**Status: design-assessment scenarios against the current implementation, not independently accepted product requirements or evaluation results.** The rows preserve interpretation distinctions for textual review. The [candidate inventory](../behavior/discovery.md#requirement-candidates) identifies where independent product acceptance remains unresolved. [Architecture](../docs/architecture.md#what-evaluation-evidence-can-establish) owns the relationship to behavioral evidence; [evaluations.md](../evaluations/docs/evaluations.md#purpose-and-evaluation-model) owns evaluation strategy and limits. These scenarios establish neither comprehensive coverage nor a new evaluation procedure.

**Non-normative reference; pending later review.** All 28 scenarios illustrate distinctions in the currently implemented operating contract. They are not requirements-derived evaluation criteria, executable test cases, behavioral evaluation results, proof of complete coverage, or an additional source of operating obligations. All 28 are preserved pending later review against the then-current requirements-led pipeline (retained here as historical context): Product requirements → candidate contract → behavioral evaluations → human review → authorized revision when appropriate. That review must determine whether each scenario belongs in requirements clarification, requirements-derived evaluation expectations, architectural explanation, or historical reference. This transfer performs no such review and establishes no accepted product intent or evaluation expectations.

The design can be assessed at two levels: whether the contract works as a self-contained set of instructions, and whether a proposed change preserves or deliberately alters the meaning of its clauses. A standalone reading tests the first; comparison of strength, trigger, scope, exception, and meaning tests the second. The detailed patterns, fields, and obligations are specified in the [current-semantic topic sections](contract-design.md#current-semantics-vocabulary-and-coverage); `AGENTS.md` remains the normative runtime authority.

| Scenario | Design distinction to assess |
| --- | --- |
| Broad objective, including a new project | The agent limits its next commitment and keeps later work at outline level until detail is needed for the next decision. Necessary depth, affected dependencies, and validation remain within scope; the breadth of the goal does not justify an unconstrained first increment. |
| Starting a task | After proportionate preliminary context gathering, assess whether the agent briefly communicates the task outline and first increment's objective and bounds before substantial work. The statement creates no approval checkpoint. It can also communicate material inquiry uncertainty and its significance, satisfying that notification without a second message. |
| Requester expectation or binding limit | “I expect a small fix” is not automatically a budget; “consult before a second increment” is a checkpoint. Material ambiguity about the intended force needs clarification. Only requester direction can revise requester-imposed limits or checkpoints; subdivision and retries do not reset them. |
| Ordinary authorized change | The agent selects a small increment and completes its checks before completing the increment, even without a chat report. Completion grants no additional permission or automatic documentation artifact; another increment can proceed under existing authorization unless a checkpoint requires a pause. |
| Completed exploration without a record trigger | Without durable material findings or significant lasting choices, no record is required merely to log the discussion. Transient task facts, such as a temporary CI outage or a failed experiment relevant only to the current attempt, follow the reporting rules only. |
| Material findings or significant proposals during review | A [finding](../contract/prerelease_AGENTS.md#32-durable-findings-and-permanent-records) needs a permanent record only when it is both material and durable, even if it comes from a discarded approach; a missing owner leads to the minimum record rather than backfill, and a prohibited write is reported as unfinished work. A concrete significant proposal ready for decision or review requires an [ADR](../contract/prerelease_AGENTS.md#33-significant-decisions-adrs) retaining the evidence that materially shaped it rather than routine PDSA history; brainstorming or a finding alone does not. Status reflects existing decision authority. Recording supplies no implementation authority or new approval checkpoint. |
| Verified factual documentation error during assessment | The agent may make an existing description of current project facts within the assessed scope match the canonical owner of that kind of knowledge, subject to scope, requester restrictions, §3.8, and tool permissions. Requirements, decision records, and protected instruction files are excluded. A verified mismatch with authoritative package configuration can justify correcting a README command; it does not authorize changing implementation, intended requirements, accepted decisions, or protected instruction files. |
| Task-relevant debt found during assessment | Evidenced debt may be recorded after considering accepted requirements and trade-offs. Any entry triggers classification-uncertainty and chat-disclosure duties. The general material-findings requirement still applies; neither classification nor recording authorizes a fix. |
| Requester or tool prohibits writes | Assessment permissions remain subject to requester restrictions (§2.1) and §3.8. An explicit ban on creating, modifying, or deleting files covers records and factual documentation corrections; tool restrictions also remain binding. Findings and proposed text can be supplied in chat within those limits, but chat does not satisfy a permanent-record obligation; the unmade record is reported as unfinished work. |
| Continued execution and reporting | Several increments can complete without a report for each one. At task completion, handoff, or when seeking input, the agent consolidates unreported work. Routine progress reports should be minimized and intermediate communication should be concise and useful. Assess a routine update against those recommendations and any requester or tool instructions; its presence alone is not an automatic violation. Required findings and records are maintained throughout. |
| Requested or required disclosures | Disclosures affecting authorization or a decision requiring requester input must precede affected work. Other information, including inquiry outcomes, should normally join the next consolidated report. Initial task framing precedes substantial work, and material inquiry uncertainty and its significance must be made clear promptly unless already communicated in the request or task framing. Reporting or notification alone creates no approval checkpoint and cannot replace required consultation. |
| Selecting and revisiting work | Plan connects the requested outcome, permission, relevant context, and evidence to a useful next increment from the first request. Assess whether that connection is understandable, including when missing evidence or consequential constraints change the next step. Study and Act inform later planning; later work stays at outline level until detail is needed and revisions stay within authorization. |
| Investigation contradicts its prediction | An evidenced answer can complete the investigation while disproving the prediction; unmet implementation behavior remains incomplete. |
| Reading existing project documentation | Reading remains context gathering without inspection-by-inspection announcements, even for a material blocking question. Inspecting relevant code, callers, tests, configuration, and directly related dependencies to establish context is also context gathering. Scope, requester limits, proportionate work, and cumulative reassessment apply to all of it. |
| Prescribed empirical operation or acceptance check | Unknown output alone does not establish inquiry. Contrast executing a settled measurement/calculation or checking acceptance with using the same procedure to investigate an unsettled approach. Classify purpose before assessing inquiry duties; a procedure neither automatically triggers nor exempts inquiry. |
| Material uncertainty about how to proceed blocks the next decision after context gathering | Promptly make the uncertainty and its significance clear unless already communicated in the request or task framing. Private planning or a retrospective report cannot replace a required timely notification. Assess actual bounded, proportionate investigation; the absence of a five-element announcement or declared effort allowance is not itself a violation. |
| The request already communicates inquiry uncertainty and its significance | No duplicate inquiry notification is required. Initial task framing, bounded conduct, sufficient evidence, cumulative reassessment, and any required consultation still apply. Coverage by the request does not authorize every consequential direction that the inquiry might suggest. |
| Inquiry exposes an unsettled consequential direction | Narrow investigation may establish facts for consultation within existing authorization. Obtain requester direction before adopting or implementing a choice requiring consultation under §2.2; merely notifying the requester or presenting evidence does not satisfy that requirement. |
| Investigation obtains sufficient evidence or reaches an increment's bounds | End inquiry when evidence is sufficient. At an increment's bounds with insufficient evidence, reassess before further investigation without claiming completion. Further attempts need justification from preceding findings and must respect authorization, limits, and checkpoints; a new advance announcement is not automatic. Report outcomes at §3.7's occasions and seek required direction before affected work. Neither an exhausted attempt nor a new increment supplies authority or resets a requester limit. |
| Acceptance-critical verification is unavailable | Sufficient alternative evidence can establish the same condition unless a specific check is required. Otherwise the condition remains unverified and validation incomplete unless validly revised. Disclosure alone does not satisfy it. |
| Equivalent evidence exists for an available acceptance check | The relevant available check must run. Equivalence alone does not permit substitution; the unavailable-check exception does not apply. |
| Known risk without an internal-quality compromise | Preserve the known risk under topic 11 without inventing a technical-debt classification. |
| Documentation-only work | Applicable methods and validation apply without requiring code or unit tests for their own sake. |
| Additional work | Reassess cumulative progress, remaining work, direction, and limits before expanding planned work or starting another increment/attempt. Each next increment needs evidence supporting its contribution and must remain within existing authorization, direction, and limits. |
| New consequential design direction or material departure | Obtain requester direction before adoption or implementation unless the specific choice is already authorized, including in a new project without established architecture. A Proposed ADR can prepare the decision; recording it or reporting later cannot supply prior authorization. |
| Handoff and resumption | Transfer meaningful results and continuation state: task objective and established direction; applicable limits/checkpoints and consumed allowances; next or unfinished increment objective/bounds/acceptance conditions; and relevant inquiry conclusions, supporting evidence, and unresolved uncertainty. A chronological activity log is insufficient. Use that state on resumption; recover missing authorization or limit state or obtain direction before dependent work. |
| Any tool fails to load the contract | The package defines no separate bootstrap stop/report/resume policy. Neither a bridge nor a README supplies one, and an unloaded contract cannot govern the failure. Use actual tool and requester instructions and distinguish loading diagnostics from demonstrated application. |
| Root and directory instruction files | Only the three root-relative contract/bridge paths receive special edit protection. Subtree rules may be added only when compatible with the root contract; the tool decides discovery, scope, and precedence, which never makes conflicting policy acceptable; encountered conflicts are reported. |

The relevant dependencies span [engineering](../contract/prerelease_AGENTS.md#12-do--perform-the-authorized-work), [validation](../contract/prerelease_AGENTS.md#13-study--assess-completion-and-learning), the [progression guards](../contract/prerelease_AGENTS.md#2-progression-guards), the [triggered rules](../contract/prerelease_AGENTS.md#3-triggered-rules) for knowledge retention and documentation, and [how the layers compose](../contract/prerelease_AGENTS.md#4-how-the-layers-compose). Method conditions, appropriate-test recommendations, required validation, documentation scope, and requester authority all contribute to the resulting behavior.

Changes to protected instruction files follow [their authorization rule](../contract/prerelease_AGENTS.md#38-instructions-protected-files-and-external-actions), including file-specific recommendations and necessary companion edits to the other two listed files; other affected documentation follows the ordinary synchronization rules. Substantive policy changes need explicit identification. An explanation or review checklist cannot substitute for the actual clauses, and a shorter contract does not by itself establish equivalent agent behavior or token savings.
