`requirements/` is the product-requirements home for coding-agent collaboration. This entry point explains the requirements domain, links to its current authoritative sources, and identifies the remaining migration work.

**[docs/product-requirements.md](docs/product-requirements.md) owns the established objectives, PR1, and product constraints; [docs/requirement-candidates.md](docs/requirement-candidates.md) owns provisional RC1–RC10 and their unresolved acceptance questions.** [docs/architecture.md](docs/architecture.md) owns the reusable operating contract's status and evidence boundaries, engineering scope boundaries, provider integration and instruction loading, design goals and boundaries, layer interaction, continuation behavior, shared current-semantics vocabulary and contract-clause coverage index, and [selected contract-design decisions and historical alternatives](docs/architecture.md#architecture-decisions), with [method-reference semantics and source assignments](docs/architecture.md#principles-and-reference-boundaries) under Crosscutting Concepts and non-normative [assessment scenarios](docs/architecture.md#assessing-design-changes) under Design Reference. [docs/contract-design.md](docs/contract-design.md) is a temporary consolidation document within the requirements project, retaining all remaining design-specific knowledge pending separately reviewed refactoring into focused requirements-domain documents. This README provides orientation and navigation; it establishes no competing requirement definitions and changes no acceptance or decision status.

## Responsibility and boundaries

The requirements project defines the collaboration outcomes wanted and why, the applicable product constraints, and which objectives and requirements are established versus provisional candidates awaiting developer acceptance. It remains independent of the current contract implementation and evaluation machinery.

The staged candidate contract expresses those outcomes as instructions. An existing contract clause establishes what the current contract prescribes; it does not by itself establish an accepted product requirement. PDSA, EARS, the three-layer contract organization, and other selected methods do not become enduring requirements merely through their use in the current design. Their present contractual force and any future authorized revision remain distinct from product-requirement acceptance.

The evaluation project's responsibility is to derive behavioral evaluations from product requirements and own the evaluation machinery and its evaluator requirements E1–E40. The current framework assesses the staged contract; explicit product-requirement traceability remains later work. Evaluation results supply evidence for review without accepting requirements or authorizing changes to them.

Development agents are governed by applicable development instructions and developer direction, not the staged candidate contract. See [development-time and runtime authority](../docs/architecture.md#development-time-and-runtime-authority) for the authority and instruction-loading boundaries.

## Remaining migration work

The established objectives, PR1, and constraints and the provisional RC1–RC10 inventory have transferred with their provenance, qualifications, and links to retained design rationale, decisions, and significant historical alternatives. The design overview, three-layer interaction, worked task example, current-semantics vocabulary and contract-clause coverage index, and selected contract-design decisions and historical alternatives have transferred to the requirements-domain architecture, together with method-reference semantics and source assignments under Crosscutting Concepts and all 28 non-normative assessment scenarios under Design Reference. The scenarios remain pending later requirements-led review. The temporary consolidation document retains its forwarding anchors and other remaining material; deletion requires a separate full-content and reference audit after review of this transfer. Further requirements extraction requires separately reviewed changes that preserve provenance and decision status, keep desired outcomes distinct from current implementation mechanisms, and preserve provisional candidates as provisional unless separately accepted.

The requirements baseline is not complete. Candidate acceptance, further requirements extraction, contract redesign, and requirement-to-evaluation traceability remain separate future work; this entry point performs none of them.
